Bun Cha

Description

What is Bun Cha?

Bun Cha is a traditional Vietnamese pork dish that is a speciality of Hanoi, the capital city of Vietnam. Seasoned pork patties and caramelised pork belly slices are served in a broth alongside rice noodles, fresh vegetables and herbs.

Bun Cha ingredients

  • Pork Belly
  • Ground Pork
  • Sugar, Black pepper, Fish Sauce, Oyster Sauce
  • Vietnamese caramel sauce
  • Shallot, Scallions, Carrot and Garlic
  • Rice vinegar

Steps

  1. Slice pork belly into thin pieces about 1½ inches (4 cm) wide. Combine half the fish sauce, sugar, oyster sauce, caramel sauce, and black pepper with half the garlic, half the shallot, and all the white scallion parts. Coat the pork belly slices thoroughly and marinate for at least 1 hour. Overnight in the fridge produces noticeably deeper flavor — my aunt always marinates hers the night before.
  2. Mix the ground pork with the remaining sauce mixture, the rest of the garlic and shallot, and all the green scallion parts. Add the cooking oil and mix thoroughly — the fat is what keeps the patties from drying out on the grill. Rest for 30 to 60 minutes, then shape into small flat patties about the size of a silver dollar. Don’t compact them too tightly; a light hand keeps the texture tender rather than dense.
  3. Peel and thinly slice the green papaya and carrot — a mandoline speeds this up considerably, though watch your fingers. If using papaya, soak the slices in lightly salted water for 30 minutes to draw out the milky sap, then rinse and drain well. Toss both vegetables with a pinch of salt, 2 tbsp sugar, and 2 tbsp rice vinegar. Let sit for 15 minutes. The pickles should be crisp, lightly sweet, and gently sour.
  4. Combine fish sauce, sugar, and water in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir until the sugar dissolves completely. Add the rice vinegar and lime juice. Stir in the minced garlic, chili, and pickled vegetables. Let everything steep together for at least 15 minutes before serving. This sauce is meant to be served warm — particularly comforting in cooler months, when Hanoians heat it up properly before bringing it to the table.
  5. Grill the Pork
  6. Cook noodles according to package directions — usually a 3 to 5 minute soak in boiling water. Drain immediately and rinse under cold running water to stop the cooking and prevent sticking. Toss with a tiny drizzle of neutral oil if you’re not serving right away. Do not let cooked noodles sit in standing water or they’ll turn soft and clump together.
  7. Place the grilled pork in a wide, shallow bowl. Pour the warm dipping sauce and pickles over the pork. Serve the noodles and herb plate alongside. To eat: pick up a bundle of noodles and a few herb leaves with your chopsticks, dip them into the bowl, and pull out a piece of pork on the way up. Or tip everything into the bowl and eat it mixed. Both are traditional. There’s no wrong method once the food is in front of you.
